Six AI apps. Three terminals. A router in a browser tab. And a notes app nobody opens. We put all of it in *one* *window,* *around* *one* *core.*

Nerve started as a weekend fix for our own messy desktop. It turned into the place where all of our AI work happens.

Why we built it

Four ideas, one core.

Neural coreScattered
The sprawl

Twelve tabs by lunchtime.

Every AI tool ships its own window, its own login and its own idea of where your work lives. By midday nobody can tell which agent changed which file.

One core

Everything connects to one place.

Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Antigravity, Ollama and ComfyUI open inside the hub with their own sessions and terminals intact. Nothing gets replaced. It all just lives together.

Every pulse is real

If it moves, it happened.

The light running across the network is a real request going out to a real model. When nothing is running, nothing moves. We never fake activity to make a screen look busy.

You hold the stop button

Nothing runs without you.

Agents ask before they edit. Every request lands in one Approvals tray, and Stop all halts every chat, terminal and worker at once until you say go again.

How to use it

Five steps to your first session.

  1. Step 1 of 5

    Install and open

    Drag Nerve into Applications and open it. You need a Mac with Apple Silicon. On first launch it checks which AI tools you already have, so there's nothing to set up for those.

  2. Step 2 of 5

    Connect your router

    Point it at your OmniRoute gateway. It tries localhost first. Your providers and models show up on the neural core as soon as it connects.

  3. Step 3 of 5

    Open your AI apps

    Go to AI Apps and pick a client. It runs inside the hub with its own terminal session. To pass a job to a different app, use Send to AI App.

  4. Step 4 of 5

    Talk or type

    Type in the composer, or hold the voice key and speak. Quick prompts sit under the input for the jobs you run every day.

  5. Step 5 of 5

    Approve or stop

    When an agent wants to change a file, the request lands in the Approvals tray with the diff. Approve it, deny it, or press Stop all.

In your pocket

The same core, on your phone.

The iOS companion connects to your Mac over Tailscale. Pull towards the centre to open the radial menu, check the router, carry on a Claude Code or Codex session, or just talk to it on the walk home.

  • Signs in automatically over Tailscale. Switch between your Mac, a Windows PC or a custom host.
  • Login tokens are stored per host and only go to hosts you've allowed.
  • Voice replies stream back, and you can cut in while it's still talking.

How we build

Four rules we don't bend.

Real data only

Every number, node and pulse comes from a real source: a call log, a session file, your vault. If we can't measure something, the screen says so.

Local first

It runs on your Mac and talks to your own tools. Your sessions and notes stay on your machine.

Your models, your keys

Bring the providers you already pay for, or run local models through Ollama. Secrets never reach the interface.

You hold the stop

Every new launch starts in normal mode. Full autonomy needs a typed confirmation, and it resets when you quit.
